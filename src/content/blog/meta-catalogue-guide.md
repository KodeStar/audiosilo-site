A familiar title can have several audiobook recordings. AudioSilo Meta's catalogue helps you compare their narrators and details, inspect the chapters in a particular recording, and plan your way through a series. It keeps each recording separate, so you can follow the voice you enjoy and see which details the catalogue still lacks.

## Find a book, series or narrator

Start at the [catalogue directory](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse). Follow the lists of [audiobooks](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse/works), [series](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse/series), or [authors and narrators](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse/people), then open an entry. Longer lists have links to the next and previous pages.

These routes give you a way to explore when you haven't settled on a search term. The directory also links to existing community character and recap guides.

## Compare recordings of the same book

On a book's page, **Compare recordings** puts the catalogued versions side by side. Each row shows the narrators, language, abridgement, runtime, release date, publisher and chapter count where those details are known. Follow a recording's link to its detailed card on the same page.

This helps with questions such as which narrator reads a version, whether its stated runtime fits the time you have, or whether the catalogue identifies it as abridged or unabridged. Missing details say **Unknown**. A missing abridgement statement stays unknown; it doesn't become an unabridged claim.

The detailed cards keep their identifiers and retailer links beside the recording they describe. A catalogue entry or retailer link doesn't confirm that a recording is currently available to buy or listen to.

## Inspect that recording's chapters

When a recording has catalogued chapters, follow its chapter count in the comparison or **Browse chapter titles, starts and durations** on its card. The chapter page names the recording and its narrators, then lists each chapter's title, start time and duration. Longer chapter lists have page links, and **Recording details** takes you back to the recording's card.

Chapter lists belong to individual recordings. If you're checking a heading or a timestamp against the audiobook you have, choose the matching recording first.

## Plan your listening through a series

Open a series and find its **Listening guide**. It lays out the catalogued works in the series' stated order, with recording choices for each one. **Narrator continuity** highlights narrators credited on more than one work among the choices on the current page, helping you look for a familiar voice across the series.

Use **Choose a recording** for each work you want to include. The **Listening time planner** adds the known runtimes of your choices into a **Chosen-list subtotal**. It waits for your choices and counts one chosen recording per work.

Treat that subtotal as a plan for the entries shown. Unchosen works, chosen recordings with unknown runtimes, and entries on other pages leave it partial. Choices apply to the current page. Overlapping series positions can include repeated coverage, and the planner flags that too.

If a work has more recordings than the guide shows, **Compare every recording** opens its book page. **No recording catalogued** means the catalogue has no recording listed for that work.

## Follow a narrator's recording credits

Open a narrator's name to see their **Narrated recordings**. Credits are grouped by series and language within the current page, with page links for longer lists. Each credit leads to the particular recording's card.

Separate recordings of the same book keep separate credits. That makes it easier to check which version someone narrated before following them to another book in the series.

## Catch up at your own pace

The [character guides](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse/characters) and [recap guides](https://meta.audiosilo.app/browse/recaps) list books with existing community material. You can also reach those guides from a book's page, or use its **Characters** and **Story so far** tabs where present.

Character descriptions and aliases stay closed until you open them. Recap passages stay closed too, with labels showing how far you should have listened before reading. Check the label before opening a passage; whole-book summary and ending rows are full spoilers.

For the catalogue's other tools, including language choices, watching a series and contributing corrections, see [Using the community metadata site](https://docs.audiosilo.app/users/community/meta-site). If you're matching these details to your own AudioSilo library, [Fix covers and series](/blog/fix-covers-and-series/) covers that workflow.
